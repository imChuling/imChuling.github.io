Second Self

Independent Project · Ongoing Interactive Vocal Performance Max for Live · FluCoMa Real-Time Audio

A singer duets with her own voice recorded earlier in the same song. No operator, no controller.

Overview

Second Self grew out of the friction of operating software while performing. It is a vocal performance system built as three Max for Live devices: it records and slices the vocal in real time, recalls earlier phrases according to the chord being played, and keeps them in time with Ableton Live. Once the take starts, nothing is operated by hand.

All output is derived from the voice recorded during the performance. The system runs on an M2 MacBook Air CPU and uses no neural models.

How it works

Segmentation and analysis. The vocal is recorded into a buffer and cut at onsets into segments of 0.4 to 4 s (FluCoMa). Each segment gets a 12-bin chroma vector, a Tonal Interval Vector and six descriptors (RMS, zero-crossing rate, crest factor, chroma entropy, chroma flux, duration). Harmonic-percussive separation stores the percussive part of each segment in a second buffer.

Indexing. The accompaniment is a four-bar chord loop. A MIDI clip of triads supplies the current chord. Each segment is stored with the chord sounding at its start, its offset from that chord’s onset in milliseconds, and its position in the verse form (A, A, AB, AB).

Recall. A segment plays back over the chord it was sung on, at its stored offset. Consecutive segments of one phrase play as a single span. When no chord clip is running, the system queries a k-d tree (k = 5) with the Tonal Interval Vector of the pitch classes just sung and ranks candidates by pitch-class conflict.

Fitting. Each recalled phrase gets a conflict score from 0 to 1 against the current chord. The score sets the depth of a pitch-class spectral mask (8192-point FFT) that attenuates partials outside the chord by up to about 9 dB. The same score moves the phrase outward in the stereo field and increases its low-pass filtering and reverb send.

Four acts

Over four acts the system shifts from a passive follower to an active musical partner. The act sets which part of the verse the second voice draws from, relative to the singer’s position. Acts advance on elapsed time, onset count, vocal density, dynamics and silence.

Research gap

OMax, Somax2 and DYCI2 recombine a performer’s material for improvisation and are often steered by an operator. The Reflexive Looper aligns a performer’s recordings to a chord grid and selects its behavior from the performer’s current playing mode.

I found little prior work that combines song form, material limited to the current performance, and no operator. In Second Self the system’s independence is also composed over the form of the song: it increases act by act. The open question is how a performer can anticipate and steer such a partner through singing alone, without breaking her expressive flow.

Limitations

  • All decisions are hand-written rules, tuned to one song with a fixed loop and verse form.
  • Chord input is a MIDI clip. The accompaniment audio is ignored.
  • The pitch shifter has no formant correction and adds 85 ms of latency at 48 kHz. Recorded voice is shifted downward only.
  • The act II shadow is heard about 130 ms after the singer (estimate): the 40 ms read offset plus the pitch shifter’s latency.
  • Onset detection can cut a sustained note in two.
  • No study with listeners or other performers has been run. An ablation mode for comparing retrieval strategies is implemented and unused.

Status

The system runs in Ableton Live 12 with my own voice and is in rehearsal. The act III sustained-note line and the stereo placement are implemented and tested offline and have not yet been run in a full take. A performance recording will be added here.

Second Self

Independent Project · Ongoing Interactive Vocal Performance Max for Live · FluCoMa Real-Time Audio

A singer duets with her own voice from earlier in the same song, with nobody operating the software.

Overview

Second Self began with a problem I kept meeting on stage: operating software while singing pulls attention away from the performance. In this system the performer only sings. There is no operator, no controller and no screen to watch.

Nothing is prepared in advance. Every sound the system makes is derived from what was sung that night: it records the voice, cuts it into phrases, and brings them back as a second voice whose independence grows over four acts. I designed and built it on my own as three Max for Live devices. It runs on a laptop CPU with no neural inference.

How it works

ListenRecord continuously, cut at onsets, describe each segment
RememberTag each segment with its chord, its offset and its place in the form
RecallReturn a phrase over the same chord, at the same offset
FitAttenuate what lies off the chord and place the voice in space

Listening. The vocal is recorded continuously and cut at detected onsets into segments of 0.4 to 4 seconds. Each segment is described by its chroma, a Tonal Interval Vector and six timbral descriptors, and is separated into harmonic and percussive parts (FluCoMa).

Remembering in place. The accompaniment is a four-bar chord loop. Each segment is tagged with the chord it was sung over, its offset from that chord’s onset, and its position in the song’s strophic form.

Recalling. A phrase returns only over the chord it was sung on and at the same offset, so it is on the harmony and on the pulse by construction. Where the system queries by harmony, it searches a k-d tree with the current chord’s Tonal Interval Vector and penalises pitch-class conflicts.

Fitting. A conflict score between phrase and chord sets how strongly a pitch-class spectral mask (FFT) attenuates the parts of the voice that lie off the chord. The same score also sets how far away the phrase is placed in the stereo field, so one measure decides which phrase returns, how much of it is left, and where it stands.

Four acts

Autonomy is composed as distance from the singer within the song’s form. In parallel, the processing removes one layer of the voice per act.

What the second voice does in each act
ActWhere it sings fromWhat leaves its voice
I · MirrorIt is silent and only records.Nothing
II · TraceIt shadows the phrase being sung, a third below, and doubles it with the previous verse’s take.The consonants
III · WallIt sings in canon one loop away, and builds an inner line from single held notes re-harmonised under other chords.The spectral body
IV · DepartureIt sings half a verse ahead of her. After she stops, it sings the verse through once and falls silent.The voice itself: pure tones follow her pitch

Research gap

Corpus-based co-improvisers such as OMax, Somax2 and DYCI2 are designed for free improvisation and are typically steered by an operator. Loop-based systems such as the Reflexive Looper align a performer’s own material to a chord grid, but treat their behaviour as fixed.

Less explored is a system for song-form performance in which the performer’s own past is the only material, nobody operates the software, and the system’s independence changes over the piece as a composed arc that the singer steers only by singing. That leads to the question I want to study next: how can a performer anticipate and steer a musical partner through singing alone, without breaking expressive flow?

Limitations

  • Decisions are rule-based, not learned, and are tuned to one song with a fixed loop and strophic form.
  • The harmony comes from a MIDI clip that follows the backing track, not from audio analysis of the accompaniment.
  • The pitch shifter has no formant correction, so recorded voice is only shifted downward. The real-time shadow arrives roughly 130 ms after the singer.
  • Onset slicing can split a sung phrase in the middle of a note.
  • The system has not yet been evaluated with other performers or with listeners. The retrieval strategies can be compared in an ablation mode, but that study has not been run.

Status

The system runs in Ableton Live 12 with my own voice and is in rehearsal. A recording of the complete performance will be added to this page.

Stack: Max for Live (Max 9), FluCoMa, JavaScript logic covered by an offline test suite of about 160 tests.

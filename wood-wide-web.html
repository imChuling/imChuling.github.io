Wood Wide Web

Interactive web audio Max/RNBO Generative score Sound design

Wood Wide Web is an interactive score for this website. The portfolio is designed as a quiet forest, with sounds created in Max/RNBO and played directly in your browser. As you explore the site, the way you browse shapes the soundscape, making each visit a little different.

This is the actual patch behind the music on the homepage, running right in your browser. You can choose a place, a time, and whether you're still or moving, then try a gesture to hear how the soundscape changes.

How to Listen

  • Pause for fifteen seconds. Birds start calling during the day, or an owl at night. They stop when you move again.
  • Explore the website. Scrolling, clicking, hovering over cards, and selecting text all trigger different sounds.
  • Spend some time in Story. Within about two seconds, deep low layers and a high shimmer open up around you.
  • Visit at different times. The soundscape follows your local time. You can also switch manually using ?time=day or ?time=night.

Interactive Sound Design

The soundscape is built around movement and stillness. User interactions trigger different synthesised instruments, while fifteen seconds of inactivity brings in birdsong by day, or a distant owl by night. The birds stop when movement resumes.

On the first visit, the website generates a four-note bird call and saves it in the browser. The same call plays on future visits, sung in the scale of the current section. Each visitor therefore has a different bird call.

The soundscape also adapts to the local time of day. Daytime uses brighter tones and birdsong, while nighttime introduces owl calls and longer, softer high-frequency sounds. The five sections use related keys and share the same instruments, with three-second transitions between sections. Story opens up within about two seconds, adding deep low-frequency layers, a high shimmer and a larger reverb.

There are eight synthesised instruments: glass celesta, kalimba, warm keys, glass, vibraphone, water drop, wood, and bird. Each note has its own amplitude and brightness envelope. Gesture velocity controls brightness, while pitch controls stereo panning, placing lower notes to the left and higher notes to the right. Higher notes are filtered to reduce harshness.

The background is based on an add9 chord with six independently modulated tones, combined with wind textures, high-register accents, and a low-frequency layer that responds to text selection.

Technical Implementation

All sounds are synthesised in Max 9 and exported as WebAssembly using RNBO. The sound engines are written in codebox~, and a build script assembles them into the Max patch.

  • Background sounds are processed through granular synthesis, light overdrive, and plate reverb.
  • Individual instruments bypass the granular processing and use only the shared reverb to preserve clear note attacks.
  • MIDI notes are sent from JavaScript to an eight-voice polyphonic subpatcher, with MIDI channels selecting different instruments.
  • Parameters such as the current section, user activity, mouse movement, and time of day are updated 20 times per second, with smoothing applied inside the patch.

JavaScript handles user interactions and parameter updates, while Max/RNBO handles sound synthesis and processing.

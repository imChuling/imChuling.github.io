Overview

Pocket Producer grew out of my frustration with unfinished songs and scattered musical ideas. In the library, a multi-agent system organizes audio clips and lyrics into cohesive projects. In an open Audiotool session, it ranks your own fragments against the arrangement, shows the evidence, and lets you preview, insert, and undo.

System

Library. Each upload is analyzed with librosa (tempo, key, 12-bin CQT chroma, onset strength) and embedded with a frozen MS-CLAP encoder. Gemini proposes a title and tags for emotion, theme, and style. Two Google ADK agents retrieve neighbors with Voyage-3 text embeddings and decide whether the fragment joins a project, with my audio fusion score as one cue.

Session ranking. Through the Nexus SDK the app reads project tempo, region positions, and up to eight audio samples. The app includes nine rankers; the three that matter most are described here.

  • Rules (default): a weighted sum of seven metadata cues, including tempo, role gap, stated intent, and recency. It needs no session audio.
  • Five-signal fusion: mean and maximum MS-CLAP cosine against the session clips, tempo, key when the intent names one, and tag Jaccard. The linear weights are trained with source-weighted Bayesian Personalized Ranking (BPR), with same-pack Freesound loops as positives.
  • Beat Fit: aligns the first ten seconds of session and candidate on the project’s beat grid, then compares chroma per beat (cosine) and onset peaks in eight slots per beat (Pearson correlation) where both sound. Signals are standardized across candidates and summed, with no fitted weights.

An insertion is one recorded DAW transaction, so undo removes exactly what it created.

Evaluation

No dataset says which of a musician’s sketches belongs in their unfinished song, so I trained and tested on a proxy: loops from the same pack count as compatible. On 121 held-out packs, fusion’s gain in pairwise accuracy over embedding cosine on hard-similar negatives was +9.9 pp [+4.4, +15.4] with MS-CLAP and source-weighted BPR, and +2.1 pp [−1.0, +5.3] with LAION-CLAP and plain BPR (95% bootstrap intervals). On the 25 query packs with no uploader shared with training, the first figure falls to +3.9 pp [−1.9, +10.3]. The gain varies with encoder, training recipe, and mined negatives, so I report it as configuration sensitivity. Small shifts in labels and audio representation change the ranking substantially, which shows how far an offline metric can sit from what a musician intends.

I then built label-free tests: change one musical property and see which scorers respond. Below, each stem is delayed four beats against its own backing.

Human in the loop

None of these scores has been checked against musician preference, so every model output can be corrected or undone: the musician can edit the generated analysis, audition a suggestion, and accept or reject it. Tag and title edits are summarized into later Gemini prompts (prompt conditioning, fixed model weights). The session log records what was shown, previewed, inserted, skipped, and undone. Refitting ranker weights from it is an offline prototype, and a guardrail rejects weights that fail a behavioral test the deployed weights pass.

Limitations

  • No listening study has been run. Grouping and edit conditioning are unevaluated.
  • The delay test measures a response to one edit, and shifting a stem also changes which audio falls inside the analysis window.
  • The onset comparison was chosen after the first results on the same recordings. The deployed rhythm measure has synthetic tests only.
  • Beat Fit ignores region loop and offset settings and cannot score fragments uploaded before it.

Pocket Producer: Session-Conditioned Retrieval as User-Controlled Proposal

Chuling Li

ISMIR 2026 Late-Breaking Demo · Abu Dhabi, UAE

Which fragments could grow into a piece of music with the ones already in a musician’s open project? This target has no direct labels at cold start, and audio similarity is only one observable signal for it. Pocket Producer, a retrieval prototype evaluated offline, treats this missing-label problem as a ranking and an interaction-design challenge. It fingerprints the open session and ranks candidates with a rules default and a selectable fusion of five interpretable signals trained on pack co-membership as cold-start weak supervision. On the Freesound Loop Dataset, the learned fusion’s apparent advantage varies with label construction, negative regime, and representation/training configuration. Pack co-membership is only a stand-in for the real target, and the conclusions shift with how the proxy is built and evaluated. We therefore surface retrieval as proposals the musician can inspect, preview, insert, and undo, not as automated decisions.
